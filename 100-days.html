A first hundred days, written before day one

The advertisement offers this role the chance to shape “the methods, networks, and activities used to identify funding opportunities and develop consortia”. Rather than describe how I would use that freedom, here is the plan, built on the call radar and on what SDU Microelectronics publishes about itself. It is written to be challenged in an interview.

The radar shows the calendar I would walk into. Several calls close in March 2027, among them Eurostars and two Horizon Europe topics, while most of this year's chip deadlines fell in September. If I start early next year, the first months therefore mean helping with what is already moving for March and building the first new consortium for later rounds.

Days
1 to 30
Pipeline, and the six research areas
Turn the radar into the shared pipeline
The radar lists calls, while the pipeline in the advertisement also holds project ideas and partners. In the first two weeks I would add those two columns, agree with the TEK Innovation funding advisers where the list lives and who edits it, and start the weekly deadline check described on the radar page.
Meet the people behind each research area
One conversation per area, and with ICELab and CryoLab, built on three questions: what would you build with the next grant, which company or partner is missing, and which deadline are you already watching. SDU Microelectronics already lists projects funded by Innovation Fund Denmark, the Independent Research Fund Denmark and Horizon Europe, so I would start from what already works.
Learn how TEK Innovation works today
Before adding anything, I want to know who writes budgets, who checks eligibility and how a researcher asks for help now. The aim is to add pipeline to the funding advisers' work without adding administration to it.
Help with what is already moving for March
Where a group is already in a consortium for a March 2027 deadline, I would help with partner contacts and the Danish national application, and I would not start new bids for those dates.
Day 30: the shared pipeline, first version, and a one page note per research area with its next idea, its missing partner and its best matching call, presented to the head of TEK Innovation and the head of SDU Microelectronics.
Days
31 to 60
First company conversations on Fyn
Ten conversations with Fyn companies in drones, robotics and sensors
The advertisement names drones, robotics, sensors and autonomous systems as the application areas to connect with the chip ecosystem, and Fyn has companies in each of them. I would start with firms whose products run on batteries or work in harsh conditions, because low power circuits, power management and reliable chips are what SDU designs.
Ask about the need before the grant
Each conversation asks what the product needs from a chip that the company cannot buy, and who in the company would sit in a project. I have sold robots to industrial buyers, so I know a company describes a need in cost, power and delivery time long before it says research.
Match each company to a researcher and a call
Where a company and a group fit, I would write a half page idea and test it against the radar. Eurostars, the EU programme for company led research, suits a Fyn SME that leads with SDU and one partner abroad, since the rules ask for partners from at least two countries.
Use what already exists
SDU Microelectronics already runs events such as Chips & Dip on 20 November 2026, and the advertisement names DkCCC as the strategic platform. I would bring companies to those rather than start a new event series.
Day 60: ten company conversations written up, and two pairs of a company and a research group, each with a named call and a half page idea.
Days
61 to 100
A first consortium outline for one real call
The call: space critical electronic components
The Horizon Europe topic HORIZON-CL4-2027-SPACE-07-83 is on the radar as a strong fit for the Space and Security area. It opens on 7 October 2027 and closes on 20 January 2028, which leaves time to build a consortium properly rather than in the last month.
The outline
The rule sets the shape: at least three organisations from three countries, one of them in the EU, and all from the EU, Norway or Iceland. The outline would name SDU's role, a European foundry or pilot line that makes the parts, a space equipment maker as the user and a radiation test partner, each as a role first and a named organisation second.
The risk, stated at the start
The list of components the topic will fund is not public yet. The outline would therefore carry two candidate SDU components and be checked against the list on the day it appears. If neither fits, the effort moves to the next Chips JU open call, which any of the six areas can enter.
Review
On day 100, a review with the head of TEK Innovation and Professor Moradi: what worked, what did not, said plainly, and the plan for the next two hundred days.
Day 100: a two page consortium outline with roles, candidate partners and open questions, ready to show to partners.

What I would not do in the first hundred days

I would not promise a company money before the call text confirms it, and I would not start a bid on a date that only one page confirms. I would not write the parts of proposals that the funding advisers already write well. I would also avoid consortia where SDU would be one partner among many with no clear role. The AI chip demonstrator call on the radar is an example, because it needs a commercial chip that already exists.

Written from public material: the job advertisement, the SDU Microelectronics pages and the call radar. Where I have guessed wrong about how TEK Innovation works today, correcting me is the conversation I am asking for.
