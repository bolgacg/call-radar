Five things to know before the table

The table uses a few funding terms. These five statements and the glossary beside them are enough to read every row.

  1. A funding call is a published invitation to apply for grant money, with a topic, a deadline and rules on who may apply.
  2. Most EU chip calls need a consortium: several independent organisations from several countries applying together.
  3. The Chips Joint Undertaking (Chips JU) is the EU body that writes the chip research calls. Its money comes from two EU programmes, Horizon Europe and Digital Europe.
  4. For many Chips JU calls the member countries add national money for their own partners. In Denmark that money comes from Innovation Fund Denmark (IFD).
  5. Deadlines move. A call can be extended on the EU portal weeks before the national page catches up.

A country outside the EU that takes part in Horizon Europe on near equal terms, such as Norway.
EEA
The European Economic Area: the EU plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway.
SME
A small or medium sized company, under 250 staff in the EU definition.
Fabless and foundry
A fabless company designs chips; a foundry is the factory that makes them. To tape out is to send a finished design to the foundry.
CMOS and qubit
CMOS is the transistor technology nearly all chips use. A qubit is the basic unit of a quantum computer.
FPGA
A chip whose logic can be reprogrammed after it is made.

      How I would keep this current

      The list starts going stale on the day it is checked, so the method matters more than this version of it.

      1. The EU portal publishes every topic as data. A weekly script would read it for each call on the list and flag any change to a deadline, status or eligibility note since the week before.
      2. Each row would carry the date it was last read against its source, and a row older than a month would be marked for a recheck.
      3. When the EU portal and the IFD page disagree, the portal sets the EU deadline and IFD sets the Danish money, and the row says which is which.
      4. Before anyone commits time to a call, I read the call text itself, not this list.
      5. The list would live where the TEK Innovation funding advisers can edit it, so it does not depend on one person.

      What this list does not do

      • The fit column is my reading, not the groups' view. Asking them is the first month of my plan.
      • Two rows rest on one source: the Chips JU quantum chip calls appear on IFD's site but not yet on the EU portal or the Chips JU site.
      • It covers public funders only. Private foundations, such as the Villum Foundation that funds SDU work today, are not in it.
      • It covers Denmark, the EU and one Nordic call. Other Nordic national funders are not checked.
      • It goes stale. Five calls on it have already moved their deadlines at least once, and more will.
